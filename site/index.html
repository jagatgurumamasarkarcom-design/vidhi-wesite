    Introduction

    A sankalp for the welfare of all

    Param Pujya Shri Shri 1008 Jagatguru Swami Virbhadranandgiriji Maharaj Mama Sarkar has begun divine sankalps for the awakening of eternal Sanatan, service to the nation and the welfare of all. Their aim is to renew the ancient traditions of Sanatan Dharma, awaken love for the nation and open the path of welfare for every living being.

    This great sankalp has three parts:

    १Rashtra seva

    Service for the unity, culture, values and welfare of the nation.

     २ॐSpiritual awakening of Sanatan Dharma

    Awakening the knowledge, culture and spiritual values of Sanatan Dharma.

     ३Welfare of every living being

    Care for nature, cows, people and every living being.
    Part 1

    Rashtra seva

    Service for the unity, culture, values and welfare of the nation

    With the blessings of Jagatguru and the inspiration of Shri Mamadev, a great campaign has been taken up for service to the nation, loyalty to the Constitution, protection of Sanatan culture and good conduct in society.

    Bharat is not just land; it is the sacred tapobhumi of rishis, munis, saints and great souls. Along with education, love for the nation, morality, values and duty are equally needed.

    • Love for the nation
    • Respect for the Constitution
    • Freedom from addiction
    • Respect for women
    • Gau sanrakshan
    • Environment
    • Awareness against crime
    “धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः”Service to the nation is the highest service
    Part 2

    Spiritual awakening of Sanatan Dharma

    Gyan, bhakti, seva and sadachar

    The rishi tradition of Bharat gave the world the Vedas, Upanishads, yoga, dhyan, seva and the spiritual life. The Sanatan Adhyatmik Jagriti Abhiyan carries these roots back to every home.

    Sanatan Dharma is not only rituals but gyan, bhakti, seva and sadachar. Society is guided away from blind faith, fear and exploitation towards worship as taught by the shastras.

    Guru-shishya parampara, the soul of Sanatan Dharma

    The guru is the door to knowledge, and knowledge is the path to the welfare of the soul.

    • Daily pooja-path
    • Yoga & dhyan
    • Jap & tap
    • Study of shastras
    • Gau seva
    • Tree planting
    • Annadaan
    “वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम्”No knowledge without the guru, no liberation without knowledge

    The katha

    Shri Veerbhadra Mahadev and Shri Mamadev

    Today, we begin this discourse by invoking Shri Veerbhadra Mahadev and Shri Mamadev.

    When Goddess Sati insisted on going to her father King Daksha’s yajna (sacrificial ritual) against the wishes of Lord Shiva, she left for the yajna. Upon reaching there, Goddess Sati saw that all the gods, sages, and celestial beings were present, but Lord Shiva had been deliberately denied a place. King Daksha openly spoke against Shiva and insulted Him. Witnessing this humiliation of her husband, Goddess Sati sacrificed her life by entering the sacrificial fire (havan kund).

    When Lord Shiva, seated on Mount Kailash, came to know of this, He was overwhelmed with terrible rage. From a lock of His matted hair, which He struck upon a rock, two divine beings emerged—Shri Veerbhadra Mahadev and Goddess Bhadrakali. Lord Shiva commanded these divine powers to go to King Daksha’s yajna, destroy the ritual, and separate King Daksha’s head from his body, offering it into the same fire. He further instructed Goddess Bhadrakali that if anyone obstructed Veerbhadra in fulfilling this vow, she should accompany him as a witness and ensure the complete destruction of the yajna.

    Obeying Shiva’s command, chaos spread among the gods. Veerbhadra Mahadev severed King Daksha’s head and offered it into the sacrificial fire. At that moment, Lord Shiva Himself arrived, saw Veerbhadra’s fierce and compassionate form, calmed him, and forgave King Daksha by reviving him with a goat’s head.

    Seeing the immense suffering of Goddess Sati, Lord Shiva lifted her body in His arms and began to wander the universe in deep sorrow. Due to His unbearable grief, turmoil spread across the three worlds. All the gods then approached Lord Vishnu and prayed to Him to free Shiva from this anguish. Lord Vishnu, using His Sudarshan Chakra, divided Goddess Sati’s body into 51 parts, which fell at various places and became the 51 sacred Shakti Peethas.

    After this, Lord Shiva blessed Veerbhadra and said that in the age of Kaliyuga, every lineage would have a Kuldevi (family deity), and the sons of that Kuldevi would be considered the nephews (bhanja) of Veerbhadra. Saying this, Lord Shiva merged Veerbhadra Mahadev back into Himself, and a Shiva Lingam was established there. This Shiva Lingam is still present today at Rishikesh–Haridwar.

    Since we are the children of our Kuldevi, and Veerbhadra Mahadev is the brother of our Kuldevi, we recognize Veerbhadra Mahadev in Kaliyuga as Mamadev (maternal uncle deity). The primary role of Mamadev is to protect nature, uphold righteousness (dharma), deliver justice against injustice, stop violence against living beings, free people from addictions, and guide them away from blind faith.

    This sacred and truthful account of Shri Veerbhadra Mahadev and Shri Mamadev is what we humbly present before you all.

    Yagna · the sacred fire

    Sit before Agni Dev with your family and a sankalp in your heart. Learned pandits of Shri Mamadev Tapobhumi perform the yagna with full vidhi, at the ashram, at your home, or in your name with video proof.

    Choose a yagna

    Why yagna

    The oldest prayer of Bharat

    From the Rigveda to today, the yagna is how a family speaks to the gods: with fire, mantra and a pure heart.

    Purifies the air and the home

    The smoke of ghee, samagri and samidh is said in the shastras to purify the air and the space.

    Your sankalp reaches the gods

    Agni is the mouth of the gods. What is offered with 'swaha' reaches the deity it is offered to.

    Peace from graha and dosh

    Yagna has been the way of the rishis to bring peace when a graha or a dosh brings trouble.

    'Idam na mama': not for me

    Every ahuti ends with 'idam na mama', this is not mine. The yagna teaches us to give.
